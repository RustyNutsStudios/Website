The service record

When your egg falls, it cracks, it shatters, and YOU DIED slams across the field while the colour drains out of it.

Then the card. The name of the egg you were. YOU DIED. Time, level, kills, coins. And the line that matters for next time: how many SHELLS you earned, the currency you keep between runs.

Under it is the new screen, the SERVICE RECORD. Your HP over time, as a graph, with the moments marked on it: a triangle where you levelled up, a square where you opened a chest, a diamond for a drop, a plus for a heal, and an X where it ended. Damage by weapon. Items taken. Chests opened. Coins earned and spent. A stamp across the middle: FELL AT THE POST. Then the quests this run, what you unlocked, the achievements, and three buttons: MENU, PLAY AGAIN, QUIT.

The old screen told you that you died. This one shows you the run. You can see the minute the line started going down, and what you did about it, or did not.

It is in the build now. The frame above is a forced death for the capture, five seconds in with nothing to its name, so do not read those numbers as a run. Yours will be longer.

The chest

A chest on the ground says OPEN. Hold it, and the ceremony starts.

A beam of light first. Then the rarity bangs in: RARE, or EPIC. An epic one gets the full treatment: a gold frame, a red flag over the chest, a sunburst behind it, and a row of soldier silhouettes marching along the bottom of the screen.

Then a tray of cards slides out. Three for a rare, five for an epic. You pick one. TAKE. In the takes so far, the epic tray held a Rocket Sled Ram, and the rare one a Ski Battalion Smock. You get one. The rest go back in the box.

Between waves there is the shop, the quieter way to kit out: weapons and items for coins, REROLL for ten, and LOCK to keep a card you like through the rerolls. The SN-42 Steel Bib was 52 coins, for three percent more defence and two percent less speed. Everything has a price, and a cost.

Coming, on the game's side: a longer build-up before the chest opens. The light, the flag and the cards are in the build now.

The drop

You start as one egg. Everything else about the squad, you call in.

Press the drop and a panel comes up: DROP REQUEST. Three recruits to choose from, each with a level and a role. In the take it was a Machine Gunner, ranged; a Marine, melee; a Scout, ranged. One button: RECRUIT.

Then the part that costs you. DEFEND THE DROP: a ring on the ground and thirty seconds on the clock. You stay in the ring while the horde comes, and the HUD counts it down, 0:30 to 0:01, with the bugs getting thicker the whole way.

At zero a red star lands on the squad, MACHINE GUNNER INCOMING, and then MACHINE GUNNER HAS LANDED. The recruit drops in and falls into line beside you.

Recruits can fall. When one does, the plate comes up, RECRUIT DOWN, then the name, and you carry on one short. A full squad is six comrades beside you. Night falls during a run, NIGHTFALL comes up as a card, and the Great Centipede is the boss that walks in with it.

Those thirty seconds in the ring are the game in small. The horde is coming either way. The question is whether what you gain is worth standing still for.
