The service record

Still coming

Every run in EGGREGIOUS ends the same way, so the screen that ends it had better be worth reading.

The old one was a scoreboard. YOU DIED in red, five numbers in boxes, the quests you ticked, three buttons. It told you what happened. It did not tell you why.

The new one is a service record. The run ends, the field goes dark, and the card comes up under the flag: how long you held, your level, your kills, the coins you banked, the bosses you put down. Below it, the record itself: your health across the whole run, drawn as one line from the first bug to the last. The dip where the storm came in. The stretch where the team held. The cliff at the end.

That line is the honest version of the run. A survivors game is twenty minutes of decisions you do not remember making, and the record keeps them for you. The next run starts from what it shows.

It is in the build now. When the playtest opens, the first thing we want from you is what your record says, and whether you agree with it.

The chest

Still coming

A chest in EGGREGIOUS is not a box you walk over. It is a ceremony, and it happens inside the fight.

You find it on the field and the first thing it does is glow. Then a beam goes up from it, and now the whole map knows where you are standing. Then the rarity bursts over it in the big letters. By this point you are in the open, the horde is closing, and the chest has not given you anything yet.

Then the cards. The items it holds come up one at a time, so you read each one while the bugs get nearer. Then TAKE, and you are back in it with more than you had and less room than you had.

There is a roll in it. Now and then a chest rolls twice and hands you two. You cannot plan for that. You can only decide, in the open, whether opening it here was worth it.

We built the ceremony because the loot screen in most games of this kind is the one moment that does not feel like the game. Ours does not pause anything. The horde does not wait for your new gun.

Rarity is real, the roll is real, and the double is rare enough that you will tell someone when it happens.

The drop

Nobody holds the line alone, and the way the team arrives is the thing we spent the week on.

You do not pick your squad from a menu before the run. You call them in. A star comes up on the HUD: INCOMING. A few seconds later the drop lands, hard, in a ring on the field, and the recruit steps out of it. HAS LANDED. Six specialists, each one arriving the same way, into a fight that is already happening.

The ring is the rule. While it is on the ground you DEFEND it, with a timer running down from eleven seconds, and the horde comes for it. Hold the ring and the recruit is yours. Lose it and the banner says RECRUIT DOWN, and you fight on one short.

It changes the shape of a run. A drop is a decision: call it in now, with the storm coming, or wait for a quiet half minute that might not come. The ring moves the fight to a spot you did not choose. The team you end the run with is the team you managed to land.

The drop panel, with the portraits, is in the build now. The timer is tuned by feel, and it will be tuned again by you.
